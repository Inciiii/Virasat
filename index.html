Our Story

I'm Naisha Garg, Class 12, student from Saint Soldiers Divine Public School, and the founder of Virasat.

I started Virasat with a simple question: why should something we treasure have to choose between being preserved and being useful?

At the same time, generations of Indian artisans are watching traditional crafts struggle to survive. Virasat connects these two worlds, giving cherished garments a new life and giving artisans fairer pay, visibility and recognition.

Sustainability

The most sustainable fabric is the one already in your almirah.

Every Virasat piece begins with a garment that already exists. No new cloth is woven for the main body, nothing is shredded or re-spun, and the hands that remake it are paid fairly. That is how we work towards two of the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

70.7 lakh tonnes

of textile waste is generated in India every year.

~45%

of the clothes and fabric people throw away is still not saved from landfill or burning.

12

UN Sustainable Development Goal

Responsible Consumption and Production

Target 12.5: by 2030, substantially reduce waste generation through prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse.

  • Garments that would sit unused for years, or be given away, go back into daily use.
  • Zero new fabric for the main body of every piece.
  • Reuse, not recycling: the fabric is never shredded, melted or re-spun.
  • Every offcut goes back to its owner.
8

UN Sustainable Development Goal

Decent Work and Economic Growth

Fair pay and steady work for the artisans who keep India's crafts alive.

  • 50% of every price goes to the artisan, where a middleman chain can leave them as little as 25%.
  • About ₹20.3 lakh earned by artisans in our first year.
  • 20 artisans with steady work in phulkari, chikankari and gota patti.
  • Every piece carries a card with the name and story of the artisan who made it.
